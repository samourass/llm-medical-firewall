# Common Cold vs. Seasonal Allergies — General Information

> SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT. General public-health information for this project's RAG
> demo only. Not medical advice, and not derived from any real patient record.

The common cold and seasonal allergies (allergic rhinitis) share some symptoms but have
different causes and typical patterns.

## Common cold

- Caused by a viral infection.
- Symptoms: runny/stuffy nose, sore throat, cough, mild fatigue, sometimes low-grade fever.
- Usually resolves within about 7-10 days.
- Not triggered by pollen, dust, or pet dander.

## Seasonal allergies

- Caused by the immune system reacting to allergens (pollen, dust mites, pet dander, mold).
- Symptoms: sneezing, itchy or watery eyes, runny or stuffy nose, itchy throat.
- Can last for weeks, often tied to a season or specific exposure.
- Fever is not typical for allergies.

## General self-care guidance

- Rest and hydration help with cold symptoms.
- Avoiding known allergy triggers, and over-the-counter antihistamines, are commonly discussed
  options for allergy symptoms; a pharmacist or clinician can advise on suitability.
- Seek care if symptoms are severe, persistent beyond the expected duration, or accompanied by
  high fever, difficulty breathing, or facial swelling.

This document is a general educational summary and does not replace advice from a qualified
healthcare professional.
